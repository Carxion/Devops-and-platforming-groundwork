# User interview guide: <platform or product name>

**Interviewer:** <name> | **Note taker:** <name, if any> | **User:** <role and team, not a name in shared notes> | **Date:** <date>

30 to 45 minutes. Ask about real recent events, not hypotheticals: what people did is more reliable than what they say they'd do. Listen more than you talk, and don't pitch or defend the platform.

## Before

- [ ] Ask permission to take notes, and say how they'll be used and shared
- [ ] Look up what the team runs and which platform capabilities it uses

## Opening (5 minutes)

- What does your team work on, and what's your part in it?
- How long have you been on the team?

## Their recent work (15 to 20 minutes)

- Tell me about the last time you <created a new service / deployed a change / investigated an incident>. Walk me through it, step by step.
  - Where did you start? What did you do next?
  - Where did you have to wait? For what, and how long?
  - What did you have to ask someone about? Who?
  - What did you look up? Did you find it?
- What's the most frustrating part of getting a change to production?
- What do you do yourself that you wish the platform did?
- Is there anything you've built yourselves because the platform didn't do it, or didn't do it well enough?

## The platform (10 minutes)

- Which parts of the platform do you use? Which do you avoid, and why?
- When something goes wrong with the platform, what happens? How do you find out, and who do you ask?
- If you could change one thing about the platform tomorrow, what would it be?

## Closing (5 minutes)

- Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
- Who else should I talk to?
- Can I follow up if I have more questions?

## After

- Write up within a day: what they did, where they got stuck, quotes, and surprises
- Note **observed** problems separately from **suggested** solutions
- Add findings where the team can see them, and tag the backlog items they affect
- Thank them, and tell them later what changed because of what they said
