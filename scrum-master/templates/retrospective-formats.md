# Retrospective formats

Vary the format every few sprints, so the team sees the sprint from a different angle. Each fits the five stages: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, close. Always start by reviewing the last retrospective's actions, and end with one to three actions, each with an owner, added to the next sprint's backlog.

## Start, stop, continue

**Good for:** any sprint; a team new to retrospectives.

Three columns: what should we *start* doing, *stop* doing, and *continue* doing? Everyone adds notes silently for five minutes, then the team groups similar notes, votes, and discusses the top three.

## Mad, sad, glad

**Good for:** a sprint with strong feelings: a hard incident, a big success, a conflict.

Three columns for what made people mad, sad, or glad. Feelings first, then ask what caused them. Watch for emotions about people rather than situations, and steer to the system.

## Sailboat

**Good for:** looking at the bigger picture after a release or a quarter.

Draw a boat heading for an island (the goal). Notes go on: the **wind** (what pushes us forward), the **anchors** (what holds us back), the **rocks** (risks ahead), and the **island** (where we want to be). Discuss the anchors and rocks first.

## Timeline

**Good for:** long or eventful sprints; after an incident-heavy period.

Draw the sprint as a line from start to end. Everyone adds events (deploys, incidents, decisions, people away) and marks how they felt (high or low). Patterns appear: "every low point follows an on-call handoff." Pair it with the sprint's data: unplanned work, cycle time, incidents.

## Four Ls

**Good for:** learning-heavy sprints: a new technology, a migration.

Four columns: what we **liked**, **learned**, **lacked**, and **longed for**. "Lacked" and "longed for" often surface missing skills, tools, or support to raise as impediments.

## Five whys on one problem

**Good for:** a single recurring problem the team keeps mentioning.

Write the problem ("Production deploys are rolled back about once a week"). Ask why it happens, then why that happens, about five times, until reaching something the team can change. Check each "why" with data where possible. Stop at causes in the system, not people.

## Closing questions

Pick one for the last few minutes:

- Rate this retrospective from 1 (waste of time) to 5 (very useful). What would make it a 5?
- One word for how you feel leaving.
- What's one thing you'll do differently tomorrow?
