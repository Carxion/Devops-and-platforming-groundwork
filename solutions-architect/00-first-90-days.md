# Your first 90 days as a solutions architect

[← Solutions architect](README.md)

A checklist for solutions architects joining a vendor, consultancy, or internal platform team: what you're offering, who you're offering it to, how engagements run, and what to have done yourself by day 90. It uses the same timing tags as the [tech lead checklist](../tech-lead/README.md):

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **W1** | First week. Meeting people and listening. |
| **30** | By day 30. Know the offering, the customers, and how engagements run. |
| **60** | By day 60. Run discovery and a design yourself, with a senior colleague reviewing. |
| **90** | By day 90. Take an engagement from discovery to an approved proposal and a clean handover. |

As with every role in this repository: **listen first.** A new solutions architect who proposes a redesign in the first customer meeting, before understanding the customer's problem or the company's offering, loses credibility with both.

---

## Before you start

- [ ] Ask whoever hired you: what does success look like in a year, which engagements matter most, and how is the role measured (wins, revenue, adoption, customer satisfaction, delivery outcomes)?
- [ ] Get the list of current and upcoming engagements, recent proposals (won and lost), and any reference architectures or standard offerings.
- [ ] Get access to the CRM or request tracker, the proposal library, the architecture repository, and a sandbox account in each cloud you'll design for.

## 1. People

- [ ] **W1** Meet the people you'll work with most: account or engagement managers, other solutions architects, delivery leads, and the product or platform teams behind what you offer. Ask what a good solutions architect does for them, and what a bad one costs them.
- [ ] **W1** Find out who reviews designs and proposals before they go out, and who can approve pricing, discounts, and non-standard terms.
- [ ] **30** Meet the delivery teams that build what gets sold. Ask which past proposals were hardest to deliver, and why.
- [ ] **30** Find out who your counterparts are on the customer side for each active engagement, and what each of them wants ([Working with stakeholders](04-working-with-stakeholders.md)).

## 2. The offering

- [ ] **30** Write down what you offer, as a customer would describe it: "move our applications to the cloud," "make our releases safe," not product or service names.
- [ ] **30** Build or deploy the main reference architectures yourself in a sandbox. A solutions architect who hasn't run the thing can't answer the second question about it.
- [ ] **30** Learn what the offering doesn't do well, and the usual workarounds. Customers find out eventually; it's better they hear it from you.
- [ ] **30** Find out how effort and cost are estimated today: the models, the rates, the contingency, and who signs off ([Estimating](03-designing-the-solution.md#estimating-effort-and-cost)).

## 3. How engagements run

- [ ] **30** Shadow at least three engagements at different stages: a first discovery call, a design review, and a proposal presentation.
- [ ] **30** Read three won and three lost proposals. Note what the winners did that the losers didn't, and any reasons the customer gave.
- [ ] **30** Follow one past engagement from proposal to delivery. Compare what was proposed with what was built, how long it took, and what it cost.
- [ ] **60** Find out where engagements usually go wrong: scope disputes, missed assumptions, unclear decision makers, handovers. Ask delivery leads and account managers separately.

## 4. Doing it yourself

- [ ] **60** Run discovery for a real engagement with a senior colleague present ([discovery guide](templates/discovery-guide.md)). Write up the business drivers, requirements, constraints, and success criteria, and confirm them with the customer.
- [ ] **60** Produce a solution design with at least two options and a recommendation ([solution design template](templates/solution-design.md)), and have it reviewed by an architect and a delivery lead.
- [ ] **60** Present a design or a demo to a customer or internal audience, and ask a colleague for specific feedback afterward ([Presenting](06-presenting.md)).

## 5. By day 90

- [ ] **90** Take an engagement from discovery to a submitted proposal ([proposal template](templates/proposal.md)), with the assumptions, exclusions, and risks written down and reviewed.
- [ ] **90** Hand over an agreed solution to a delivery team with a handover session and document ([delivery handover](templates/delivery-handover.md)), and check in with them a few weeks later.
- [ ] **90** Add something reusable to the team's library: a reference architecture, a discovery question set, a proposal section, or a demo.
- [ ] **90** Share a summary with your manager: what you've learned about the customers and the offering, what's working in how engagements run, and what you'd change.
